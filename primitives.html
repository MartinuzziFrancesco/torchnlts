Primitives#

This page lists every public torchnlts primitive with its gradient class (exact, smooth, piecewise, implicit, or none) and the reference(s) it’s validated against. Updated in the same commit that adds or changes a primitive.

Conventions. Trajectories are [T], [T, D] or [B, T, D] tensors, numpy arrays or pandas objects. Integer input is computed in the default floating dtype; float16 and bfloat16 are computed in float32 and, unless a docstring says otherwise, cast back; complex input raises ValueError. Exceptions: delay_embedding(), random_shuffle_surrogate() and random_shift_surrogate() only index their input, so they keep any dtype, integer and complex included; lyapunov_spectrum() needs a float32 or float64 state (QR has no half-precision kernel) and step_jacobian() a real floating one. Parameters with a default value are keyword-only. Scalar integer parameters such as embedding_dim, delay and theiler_window must be integers: a float (even 2.0) or a string raises ValueError, and so does None unless it is the parameter’s default, where it selects the default behavior (max_lag of zero_one_growth_rate(), num_exponents of lyapunov_spectrum(), library_size of cross_map_estimate(), exclusion_zone of matrix_profile(), num_neighbors of eckmann_spectrum()). The swept integer parameters (k of higuchi_length(), lag of power_variogram(), window_size of dfa_fluctuation(), k_values of the Rosenstein and Kantz estimators) take a number or a 1-D sequence of integer-valued numbers: [4.0, 8.0] is accepted, [4.5] raises ValueError; the scales of the multiscale entropies are integers. A result evaluated over a swept parameter (radii, scales, window sizes, lags, …) has the sweep on its first axis: [K] for one trajectory, [K, B] for a batch (so autocorrelation() and auto_mutual_information() give [max_delay + 1] or [max_delay + 1, B]). cross_map_estimate() returns one estimate per embedded point with the rank of its estimated series ([N], [N, D] or [B, N, D]), and ccm_skill() reduces the time axis ([], [D] or [B, D]).

Numerical range limits. Outside these, results or gradients can be inf, NaN or inaccurate even when the exact answer is representable:

  • Magnitudes within a few orders of the dtype’s largest finite value (about 1e38 for float32, 1e308 for float64): intermediate sums, squares and differences can overflow.

  • Magnitude ratios beyond about 1e30 within one series: the small values are lost relative to the large ones.

  • Distance-based primitives (everything built on pairwise_distance(): neighbor searches, recurrence, correlation sums, entropies, Lyapunov estimators, …) raise coordinate differences to the power norm_order. In float32 with norm_order=2, distances more than about 1e19 times smaller than the trajectory’s largest magnitude underflow to zero; use float64 for such data. Larger norm_order narrows this range to about 2**(-126 / norm_order) in float32 (2**(-1022 / norm_order) in float64); norm_order=float('inf') (max-norm) and norm_order=1 are not affected.

  • Absolute accuracy is about the working dtype’s epsilon times the series’ largest magnitude. A result far smaller than that and resolved only by cancellation (e.g. a DFA residual of 0.5 next to a 1e20 spike in float64) is not accurate. Katz, DFA, the variogram and the 0-1 test compute in float64, or in float32 on Apple MPS (which has no float64), where the limits above are those of float32.

Non-finite input. Higuchi, Katz, Petrosian, DFA, the variogram, the 0-1 test, scaling_slope(), Rosenstein, Kantz, Eckmann, the correlation sums (and so sample entropy, multiscale and composite multiscale entropy), approximate, fuzzy, permutation and dispersion entropy, mutual information (both estimators), conditional mutual information, transfer entropy, hard_fnn(), cao_e1(), cao_e2(), autocorrelation() and auto_mutual_information() return NaN for any batch row containing NaN or inf instead of raising, so they work under torch.vmap. estimate_delay() returns -1 for such a row. Lempel-Ziv complexity marks such a row too, with -1 for the integer count (NaN with normalize=True), but it runs a Python loop per row and does not work under torch.vmap. The entropy functions check the whole row, including samples that enter no embedded window (a large delay) or that coarse_grain drops; mutual_information ignores samples beyond the common length of its two inputs. cross_map_estimate() marks single estimates instead: an estimate is NaN when its embedded point has a non-finite coordinate, when fewer than embedding_dim + 1 neighbors are left by the Theiler window and library_size, or when a neighbor’s target is non-finite. ccm_skill() is NaN for each [B, D] entry whose estimate or actual is constant or non-finite. lyapunov_spectrum() and step_jacobian() give NaN for a state, or a row of a batched state, containing NaN or inf; step_fn is evaluated at the zero state in its place. The torchnlts.sciml losses give a NaN loss for a batch row whose predicted or target trajectory (LyapunovLoss: initial state) contains NaN or inf, so the "mean" and "sum" reductions are NaN too. surrogate_test() returns a NaN p-value for a row whose trajectory has a non-finite entry or whose data or any surrogate statistic is non-finite (NaN or inf); the shuffle and shift surrogates move non-finite values like any other. pairwise_distance() returns NaN for every pair involving a point with a NaN/inf coordinate. Other rows (or pairs, or estimates) and their gradients are unaffected, and a non-finite row receives a zero gradient. Exceptions: Petrosian has no gradient at all, and a result that is NaN for another reason (e.g. a series too short for the requested sweep) is not connected to the input’s graph. The hard recurrence matrices with recurrence_rate, local_rate or scale compute their thresholds over the finite distances of each batch row (a row with none gives an all-False matrix) and work under torch.vmap.
